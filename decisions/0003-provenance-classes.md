# ADR-0003: Provenance classes replace the closed-API training ban

**Status:** Accepted (2026-09-20) · **Supersedes** the "bright line" in
ADR-0002 §Delegation ("closed-API outputs … never into the training corpus")

## Context

ADR-0002 drew a hard line: no closed-API outputs in training data, ever.
Since then the tooling matured (license ledgers, purge-by-source in
`data/real/`, engine-scoped provenance in the factory), and closed judgment
models (TypeSafe Jev) became the best available labeler for semantic
phenomena (silence meaning, addressee, turn labels) that open models label
poorly. The blanket ban now blocks the strongest labeling path
(LLM-assisted labeling is also standard industry practice).

## Decision

Replace the ban with **provenance classes**:

1. **open** — Apache-2.0/MIT/CC-BY/PD outputs (Qwen, MiniCPM, Chatterbox,
   whisper, LibriSpeech, C-SPAN-PD…). Unrestricted use.
2. **closed-labeled** — training data whose *labels or supervision signal*
   were produced by a closed model (Jev, Kimi, ElevenLabs…). Permitted;
   every such stream carries provenance tags at creation (source model,
   date, question set) via the existing ledger machinery.
3. **closed-content** — raw content (text/audio) generated by a closed API
   entering training directly. Permitted only with an explicit go from the
   project owner, recorded per stream.

## Consequences

- Jev may label training data (Layer-2 corpus semantics, bank audits,
  sampler weights) — tagged `closed-labeled`.
- Purge-by-provenance remains possible because every stream is tagged at
  creation; a future commercial release can identify and re-derive any
  closed-derived supervision if ever required.
- Kimi/ACP stays the delegation brain; ElevenLabs content, if ever used,
  is class 3 (owner sign-off per stream).
- docs/02-data.md and notes-typesafe-jev.md updated to match.
