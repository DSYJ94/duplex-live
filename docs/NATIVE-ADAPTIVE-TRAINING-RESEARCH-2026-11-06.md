## Evidence that determines the next decision

The [complete64 review](NATIVE-ISAMBARD-COMPLETE64-REVIEW-2026-11-06.json) reports main/control word–audio agreement of 18/32 versus 13/32, the necessary quiet-before-cue/voiced-after-cue condition in 4/4 versus 2/4, and correct lookup completions of 2/4 in both delegation and pending families in each arm. These are diagnostic signals, not semantic correctness or naturalness approval. Cancellation had **zero eligible cases in either arm**: malformed requests returned immediately, leaving nothing pending to cancel. Repairing valid request formation and testing genuinely pending work precede a conclusion about cancellation capability. This inspected, one-seed cohort is now a regression set.

The [data-versus-recipe review](NATIVE-ISAMBARD-DATA-VS-RECIPE-REVIEW-2026-11-06.json) records 240 TRAIN/64 DEV scenes, with only 32 ordinary TRAIN and 12 ordinary DEV scenes. The roughly 698M trainable broad LoRA plus control adapter is already a large intervention. The encoder, base LLM and TTS remain frozen. One archived factual regression predates this capacity expansion; neither more parameters nor smaller learning rate has been established as its remedy.

[NativeOpal](../training/venus/native_optimizer.py) owns one wrapped update and one scheduler step, verifies the saved update index and preserves complete OPAL state. [The adapter](../training/venus/opal_adapter.py) pins upstream commit `8b16759a3e9930916ed582e28068267859f935f8`, permits TRAIN/DEV numeric aggregates only, and defaults to KEEP with zero agent budget. [large_train.py](../training/venus/large_train.py) explicitly selects that policy. The continuation review records nominal LR `5e-6` with an inherited `0.5` updater multiplier: the effective update schedule must include both. **KEEP preserves an existing program; it is not a currently operating code-writing agent.** Remote recovery progress is deliberately left to the executing parent agent, rather than inferred from these local documents.

## Five priorities

### 1. Prefer targeted coverage and ordinary retention over another capacity expansion

**Project inference:** the current mixture and recurring invalid requests give a more concrete next hypothesis than insufficient parameter count. Mine TRAIN failures into a short queue: ordinary factual/conversational error, unwarranted delegation, malformed request, wrong/missing result use, premature speech, failed pause/resume, and eligible cancellation failure. Store the earliest causal event, distinct-scene recurrence, source/checkpoint/data hashes, proposed correction and unresolved uncertainty. Infrastructure exceptions and audio rendering defects get engineering labels, not invented language targets.

Add **diverse, verified examples**, not duplicates of the inspected benchmark: ordinary answers that require no worker, valid complete worker arguments, result-grounded spoken answers, and cancellation while an actual task remains pending. Include benign contrasts so the repair cannot be learned as “always delegate” or “always remain silent.” Preserve ordinary KL/hidden retention and all current objective components. The right size is the next admitted, auditable tranche; 20,000 allocated GPU-hours is not evidence that a tiny repetitive corpus should receive thousands of epochs.

MiRL means **Misbehavior-driven RL**, not a generic meta-optimizer. It profiles recurring errors, starts RL continuations immediately before them, and rewards task success jointly with avoidance of the targeted behavior. Its Qwen3.5-4B terminal-agent study reports 43–57% fewer measured misbehaviors on unseen prefixes and 51.8% versus 47.4% on Terminal-Bench Lite. That supports testing targeted correction, not transferring those effect sizes to voice. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.35697v1), [official code](https://github.com/runchu-tian/MiRL).

**SFT now, RL later:** supervised corrected voice episodes are a MiRL-inspired adaptation. The released implementation is GRPO-style terminal RL with four full-weight learners, four rollout engines and a separate behavior judge; it is not a drop-in SFT or speech module. Native suffix RL would additionally require reproducible audio/KV/parser/worker/cancellation/clock state, native log probabilities and a trustworthy task-plus-behavior reward. Silence must not earn task success. Do not install that stack to complete the present run. [Released training script](https://github.com/runchu-tian/MiRL/blob/main/scripts/train.sh).

### 2. Use one explicit data/recipe/runtime decision table at each completed epoch

**Proposed policy, not a causal diagnosis:** assess the following at existing epoch boundaries, with family losses and free-running behavior together.

| Observation | Next intervention to test |
| --- | --- |
| TRAIN improves; ordinary or other DEV/free-running behavior worsens | Broaden retention/error contrasts or review regularization; do not automatically add epochs/capacity. |
| TRAIN and DEV improve; behavior improves without retention loss | A bounded continuation may be useful within its admitted limit. |
| TRAIN and DEV both stall; gradients/updates are finite and coverage is adequate | Test one LR/schedule or objective-balance change from a preserved checkpoint. |
| Large gradient spikes or unstable update/weight ratio | Diagnose the offending group, normalization and effective LR before adding data. |
| Correct generated words but wrong/missing speech | Investigate acoustic conditioning/rendering/runtime. Do not relabel correct text to match broken audio. |
| Invalid requests prevent pending work | Fix/teach valid request formation; evaluate cancellation only over eligible pending tasks. |

Keep the inspected64 unchanged for regression checks. Freeze a separately authored, uninspected cohort **before** selecting the next candidate; separate task structures, wording, synthetic worlds, audio and answers from training. Report both task correctness and text/audio agreement. If the fresh cohort informs another repair, retire it to regression status and obtain a new final cohort.

### 3. Make reactive optimization bounded and attributable

OPAL is **OPtimizer with an Agent in the Loop**: an agent rewrites analysis and update code during one run. Its paper evaluates language modeling and image generation with recipes not tuned for the target; it supplies motivation for reactive schedules, not a duplex or SFT efficacy result. The arXiv entry is now v2, revised 4 November. [Authors' explanation](https://xingjianbai.com/reactive-optimizers/), [paper/version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33826).

**Proposed local policy:** finish the data comparison with KEEP unchanged. If evidence subsequently points to optimization, permit at most two recorded decisions at epoch boundaries in one fixed-data run. First restrict the change to a global LR multiplier in `{0.5, 1.0, 2.0}` relative to the actual baseline effective schedule, with no increase beyond the separately admitted recipe ceiling. Retain gradient clipping, objectives, data, trainable tensors and total updates. Log actual per-group LR, update/weight ratio, gradient norms and family metrics; save the predecessor and complete Adam/scheduler/RNG/OPAL state before a change. These bounds are an experimental proposal, not an OPAL-proven optimum or an instruction to change the recovering run.

A controller that changes data, loss, capacity and optimizer together cannot answer which helped. Full agent-driven optimizer-code rewriting should be a later separately scoped comparison with finite waiting/decision/token limits; the current zero API budget remains zero. The pinned README explicitly warns that an agent can worsen a run and that later failures are not fully rolled back. Do not mistake program version retention for a complete training rollback guarantee. [Pinned upstream code and limitations](https://github.com/xingjian-bai/opal/blob/8b16759a3e9930916ed582e28068267859f935f8/README.md).

### 4. Test single-job multi-GPU throughput before accepting indefinite one-GPU training

**Judgment:** the existing 20,000 GPU-hour award makes a short, single-node data-parallel experiment worthwhile once the current continuation is safe. It may buy useful wall-clock speed; it does not automatically buy GPU-hour efficiency. This is neither implemented nor qualified in the native trainer. The pinned OPAL release documents single-node DDP support, while excluding multi-node, FSDP and DeepSpeed. That is upstream support, not proof for this recurrent native-BPTT graph. [Pinned OPAL README](https://github.com/xingjian-bai/opal/blob/8b16759a3e9930916ed582e28068267859f935f8/README.md).

Use one sequential throughput experiment, with no overlapping training jobs: one GPU/microbatch1/accumulation4 versus two GPUs/microbatch1/accumulation2; only consider four GPUs/microbatch1/accumulation1 if the first result justifies it. Keep **global effective batch4**, objective normalization, ordered global examples, complete sequence BPTT, precision, LR and optimizer-update count unchanged. Do not apply linear LR scaling when global batch is unchanged. DDP synchronizes gradients but does not shard inputs for the user; its averaging also requires correct loss scaling. [PyTorch DDP documentation](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.15/generated/torch.nn.parallel.DistributedDataParallel.html).

Before timing, verify a full wrapped-state restore on every rank, canonical parameter mapping, per-rank RNG and the exact global sampler cursor; compare a few equivalent updates within declared numerical tolerances. Do not assume bitwise trajectory equality after changing rank count. Clip once after global reduction; synchronize OPAL program/observations and ensure one phase update, scheduler advancement and authoritative checkpoint. Check dynamic/unused parameters and activation-checkpoint behavior rather than setting `static_graph=True` by guesswork. Preserve diagnostic checkpoints separately so this test cannot advance production state accidentally.

Measure steady-state seconds/update, startup/checkpoint overhead, examples or audio-frames/second, peak memory and total allocated GPU-hours. Let `S = T1/TN`: GPU-hour cost per equal work changes by `N/S`; two GPUs at 1.6× speed cost 1.25× GPU-hours. Predeclare a practical threshold, for example at least 1.5× speedup on two GPUs with no more than 1.35× GPU-hours, plus successful restore. Treat these as proposed tradeoffs, not measured results. Compare allocation/charging rules when implemented; do not assume the award requires using every GPU immediately.

### 5. Reserve direct weight surgery for a localized defect

The 2 November **Sparks of Weight-Space Programming** artifact is an authors' research blog with released experiment logs/code, not a general-purpose Venus editing package. In its Qwen3.5-0.8B example, direct edits removed a looping-related direction from two MLP projections; reported MATH-500 rose from 7.9% to 48.1%. Its non-looping Gemma3-270M follow-up showed no established math gain. This distinction argues for repairing a demonstrated bottleneck, not expecting edits to manufacture missing knowledge. [Primary report, experiment8 and follow-up](https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/), [official logs/code](https://github.com/RikiyaT/lm_understand-logs), [released model and hash-verifiable edit](https://huggingface.co/RikiyaT/Qwen3.5-0.8B-weight-space-programming).

**Venus extrapolation:** inspect a disposable checkpoint only if the same stereotyped failure recurs across diverse TRAIN cases and activation/ablation evidence localizes it. A plausible candidate would be a repeated pathological activation/output loop, not “low correctness” in general. Require an exact sparse diff, bounded norm, sham edit, restore test and frozen independent behavioral/retention evaluation. Small edit size does not guarantee small behavioral damage. Do not transplant Qwen's layers/directions into Venus, merge arbitrary adapters, or alter the active checkpoint. Current evidence favors data and ordinary retention ahead of this experiment.

## Minimal next experiment and stopping rule

**Nothing in this note blocks the authorized resume.** Its next epoch evaluation is already the minimal experiment about whether additional fixed-data training helps. Resume from the executing agent's independently verified durable state; do not repeat saved updates or infer durable progress from console output. A Slurm polling timeout is infrastructure evidence, not optimizer or model failure.

After that result, the smallest useful **data-only paired comparison** proposed here is one fixed epoch per arm, run sequentially from the same saved checkpoint and full optimizer state:

1. Freeze 96 admitted new TRAIN scenes: 48 ordinary/non-delegation contrasts, 24 valid request/result-use scenes and 24 scenes with genuinely pending work at cancellation. Use distinct situations and matched controls; preserve the existing 240-scene TRAIN corpus and all original labels/folds.
2. Arm A sees the original240 once plus96 preselected old rehearsal exposures. Arm B sees the original240 once plus96 new scenes. Both see336 scene exposures, effective batch4 and therefore84 optimizer updates; match frame/token budget and family weights as closely as practicable, record any residual mismatch, and keep the optimizer policy/capacity/objectives fixed. This estimates the value of the proposed new-data mixture versus spending comparable work rehearsing current data; it does not isolate every component of that mixture.
3. Before training, freeze48 fresh evaluation scenes:16ordinary,8quiet,8delegation,8eligible cancellation,4pause and4pending. Keep their contents out of the controller and repair queue. Evaluate both arms on these and the unchanged regression cohort, reporting paired disagreements, semantic task success, cancellation eligibility, spurious-delegation rate, word/audio agreement and latency. This is a small screening experiment, not sufficient by itself for product adoption; unchanged native speech/behavior and owner gates still apply.

**Do one intervention, one complete readout, then decide.** No automatic capacity escalation or extension from CE alone. Stop a candidate on non-finite updates, failed restore or reproducible retention/behavior damage. If the data arm does not help its targeted failures while preserving ordinary behavior, inspect that finite result before generating another tranche; do not begin a grid of new optimizers. For a new DDP branch, stop after one minimal compatibility repair plus one bounded timed comparison if parity/restore fails or the predeclared throughput tradeoff is missed; keep the proven single-GPU route available. Bound further literature work to a specific unresolved implementation question and one decision-changing primary source, rather than delaying the next authorized complete training result.
